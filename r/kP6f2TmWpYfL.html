What's holding the site back

AreaFinding
Buttons and links that go nowhere A customer who taps your email address or your street address gets nothing, and that is someone ready to reach you walking away. It also makes the site read as unfinished, to visitors and to Google. We found 20 links like this, including the menu, the services link, your email, and your Redpath Ave address.
Which homepage Google trusts Google has to guess which version of your homepage is the real one, so the page you care about most can compete against copies of itself. The list of pages you hand Google is in place, 42 pages on it, but the homepage is missing the small marker that says this is the one that counts.
Old admin door left open Your admin login page is open to the public and can show up in Google under your own name. That looks sloppy to anyone who stumbles on it and invites strangers to poke at it. It should be shut.
One unsecured loose end The site runs on a secure connection, but one small piece still loads the old, unsecured way. It undercuts the padlock you already paid for, and it is a quick fix. We found exactly one item doing this.
Photos and phone speed Your photos are heavier than they need to be, which slows the site on a phone, and most people looking for a mover are standing on their phone when they search. Fourteen images are in older, bulkier formats, and 3 of 26 have no written description for Google to read. The good news is the page itself answered fast when we tested it.
How you show up in search results This part is done right. Your page title says exactly what you do and where, the text under it in Google is the right length, and links shared to social media show a proper preview. Nothing to fix here.
Homepage content Your homepage has real substance, over a thousand words Google can actually read, and your business details are labelled behind the scenes so Google knows who you are and where you work. One easy add later, a question and answer section, which is the cheapest way to take up more room in search results.
Trust signals The things that make a visitor feel safe booking you are in place. Keep them there.
Your Google profile Your website and your Google business profile tell the same story, which is exactly what Google wants to see from a local company. No action needed.

Twenty of your links, including your own email address and street address, go nowhere when clicked, and that is the biggest thing standing between a visitor and a booking.

What clean SEO is worth

When someone in North Toronto needs a mover, they search on their phone, pick the company that looks most put together, and try to make contact right then. Your site does the hard part already, it reads well and looks credible, then the email and address links fail at the exact moment someone tries to reach you. Fixing the links means people already on your site can actually contact you, and the cleanup behind the scenes helps Google send more of them. For a moving company, one saved enquiry is a whole household's job.
