What's holding the site back

AreaFinding
How Google finds your pages Google was never handed a list of your pages, so it wanders the site and hopes it finds everything. On top of that, your homepage never tells Google which version of itself is the official one, so the page you care about most can end up competing against copies of itself. Both fixes are quick, and both are foundational to everything else.
Buttons that send customers away Twelve of your main buttons send visitors off to places like Facebook instead of to your own pages, which is a customer walking out the door right before they call. Another four links point to pages that no longer exist, twenty more, including your Service Areas menu link, do nothing when clicked, and two pages share the same name so Google cannot tell them apart. We checked 60 links across 12 pages to confirm all of this.
Your star rating in Google The site is currently telling Google its own star rating, and Google treats self-reported ratings as against the rules. The usual penalty is losing the stars beside your name in search results, one of the few things that makes a listing stand out. Removing the self-reported rating protects the stars you have actually earned.
An old door left open The login page for the back of your website is open to the public and can show up in Google under your own business name. That looks unfinished to a customer and invites break-in attempts from everyone else. It should be closed off from search.
Speed on a phone Most people who need an electrician are searching from a phone, and nine of your photos are heavier than they need to be, which slows the site down for exactly those people. Three photos also carry no written description, which is how Google understands what a picture shows. The good news is the page itself answered quickly when we tested it, so lighter photos would make a fast site genuinely fast.
Telling Google where you are Your site never states your name, address and phone number in the format Google reads by machine, and there is no map on the site, both standard signals for a business that wins work by neighbourhood. Oddly, the one machine-readable label the site does carry tells Google the page is about a book, not an electrical contractor. For a local trade, this is the cheapest credibility there is.
Site security The padlock is in place and everything on the site loads securely. Visitors and Google both see a safe site. Nothing to do here.
Page names and previews Your pages are named properly for search, the homepage has a clean summary ready for Google to show, and links shared on social media preview correctly. These basics are done right, which is more than most local sites manage.
Homepage copy Your homepage carries 1,786 words of real, readable content that Google can see, which is a genuine asset most electricians never build. Adding a short question-and-answer section would give Google even more of your page to show searchers. Once the issues above are fixed, this copy can start pulling its full weight.

Google is guessing its way around your site because nobody ever handed it a list of your pages, and guessing costs you calls.

What clean SEO is worth

When someone in Toronto needs an electrician, they are usually standing there with their phone, they search, and they call whoever looks credible near the top. Right now Google is working from an incomplete picture of your site, and some of the visitors you do win are being handed to Facebook by your own buttons. Fixing this does not need new content or a redesign, the 1,786 words on your homepage are already there. It means the site you have already paid for finally sends the calls to you instead of leaking them.
