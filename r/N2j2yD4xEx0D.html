What's holding the site back

AreaFinding
Buttons and links that go nowhere A customer who taps your phone number, your email, or your address on the site gets nothing, and someone with a burst pipe does not tap twice, they call the next name. Twenty links across the site currently do nothing when clicked, and eleven of your main buttons send people off to places like Facebook instead of to your own pages. Six pages are also missing the main headline that tells Google what they are about, and two pages share the same name in Google's eyes.
Old pages still open to the public Google can list your old admin login page and a leftover first-draft page under your business name, right next to the real site, which looks careless to anyone who lands on them. One of your pages also fails outright with a server error when it loads. These doors should be shut so the only thing anyone finds is the site you actually want them to see.
Which homepage Google trusts Google has to guess which version of your homepage is the official one, so the page you care about most can end up competing against copies of itself. The good news is you already hand Google a proper list of your pages, 22 of them, so this is a small and quick fix.
Your map and location There is no map of your location anywhere on the site, and for a local trade that map is one of the standard signals Google uses to decide who shows up when someone searches in their neighbourhood. Adding one is a small job with a real payoff for local searches.
Site security Your site is properly locked, the padlock both customers and Google look for, and everything on it loads securely. Nothing to fix here.
How you look in search and when shared Your homepage carries the right name and a well-sized description into Google, and links to your site preview cleanly when someone shares them in a text or a post. This is set up well.
Trust signals The trust markers customers scan for before they call are in place. Keep them where they are.
Speed Your homepage answered in about a third of a second on our check and the page itself is lean. Visitors are not left waiting on you, which matters most for people searching on a phone.
Homepage content Your homepage carries 800 words of real copy that Google can read, and the behind-the-scenes information that tells Google who you are is already in place. One easy win is still on the table: a short question-and-answer section, the cheapest way to take up more space on the results page.

The phone number on your own website does nothing when a customer taps it, and it is one of twenty links on the site that go nowhere.

What clean SEO is worth

Plumbing is bought in a hurry. When a pipe bursts, people search on their phone and call the first company that looks open and legitimate, so every dead link between them and your number is a job that goes to someone else. Fixing the links, shutting the old pages, and pointing your buttons at your own pages means the site you already paid for finally does its one job, making the phone ring. The foundation here is strong enough that this is tightening, not rebuilding.
